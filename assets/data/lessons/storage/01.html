Storage has layers. A block device is a disk or partition. A filesystem is the structure written onto it (XFS by default on RHEL). A mount attaches that filesystem to a directory in the single tree.

terminal · see all three layers
lsblk -f
blkid
findmnt
df -hT

lsblk -f shows the device tree, filesystem type, label, UUID, and mount point. blkid queries filesystem signatures directly. findmnt shows the mount tree, including source, target, and options. df -hT shows capacity by mounted filesystem.

LayerQuestion it answersTool
Block deviceWhat disks and partitions exist?lsblk
FilesystemWhat is formatted on it?blkid, lsblk -f
MountWhere is it attached and with what options?findmnt, mount
ConsumptionHow much is used and by whom?df, du

An unmounted filesystem is invisible to normal path-based tools. A mounted filesystem's root hides whatever was previously in the mount point directory. This is why mounting over a non-empty directory appears to "lose" files until you unmount.
