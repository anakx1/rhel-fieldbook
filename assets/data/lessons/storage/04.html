Many RHEL systems put storage behind LVM (Logical Volume Manager). You do not need to build it yet, but you need to recognize it.

terminal · inspect LVM
sudo pvs
sudo vgs
sudo lvs
lsblk

Think of it as three layers: physical volumes (PVs) are disks or partitions; volume groups (VGs) pool that space; logical volumes (LVs) are flexible block devices carved from the pool. The filesystem sits on the LV. This is why an LV can be extended without repartitioning a physical disk.

To extend an LV and grow XFS in one supported operation on RHEL 9+:

terminal · extend an LV and filesystem
sudo lvextend -r -L +5G /dev/vg_data/lv_app
df -h /app

The -r flag resizes the filesystem with the volume. XFS can grow online but cannot shrink. This asymmetry is a design constraint, not an inconvenience to work around with force. Plan capacity before creation.

Later levels cover PV/VG/LV creation, LVM snapshots, NFS, and Stratis. For now, be able to answer: is this filesystem on a partition or an LV, and is there free space in the volume group?
