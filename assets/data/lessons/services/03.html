systemd captures service output in the journal. journalctl reads it with filtering that flat files cannot match.

terminal · journal queries
journalctl -u sshd
journalctl -u sshd --since "1 hour ago"
journalctl -u sshd -f
journalctl -p err -b
journalctl --since today --until "10:00"

-u filters by unit, --since and --until narrow time, -f follows, -p err filters by priority, and -b limits to the current boot. -b -1 reads the previous boot if persistent storage is enabled.

By default, the journal may be volatile and live in /run/log/journal. To retain logs across reboots, create /var/log/journal and restart systemd-journald. Capacity is managed by journald.conf; on a busy host, plan disk usage deliberately.

Traditional text logs are still useful. rsyslog may write /var/log/messages, /var/log/secure, and application-specific files. The journal often has the same events, with more structure.
