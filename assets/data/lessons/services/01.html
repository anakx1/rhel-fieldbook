systemd manages units: services, sockets, timers, mounts, targets, and more. A unit is described by a file, usually under /usr/lib/systemd/system/ for packages or /etc/systemd/system/ for local overrides.

Unit typePurposeExample
.serviceA long-running daemon or oneshot task.sshd.service
.socketSocket activation; starts a service on demand.sshd.socket
.timerScheduled activation, a modern cron alternative.dnf-makecache.timer
.mountA filesystem mount managed by systemd.home.mount
.targetA grouping or boot milestone.multi-user.target

Active means running now. Enabled means configured to start at boot. They are independent. A service can be active but not enabled — it will disappear after reboot. A service can be enabled but failed — it tried to start and did not.

terminal · ask the three questions
systemctl status sshd
systemctl is-active sshd
systemctl is-enabled sshd
