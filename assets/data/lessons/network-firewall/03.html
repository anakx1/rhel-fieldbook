firewalld filters traffic by zone. A zone is a policy bucket attached to interfaces or sources. Runtime rules are active now; permanent rules are saved and become active after --reload or a restart.

terminal · inspect and open
firewall-cmd --get-active-zones
firewall-cmd --list-all
sudo firewall-cmd --permanent --add-service=http
sudo firewall-cmd --permanent --add-port=8443/tcp
sudo firewall-cmd --reload
firewall-cmd --list-all

Prefer services over raw ports when firewalld knows the service. A service definition carries every required port and helper. Use ports when the application is not packaged with a service definition or the port is genuinely custom.

RHEL 9 vs RHEL 10

firewall-cmd is stable across both releases. RHEL 10's nftables backend means old iptables inspection habits may mislead you. Use firewall-cmd for policy and nft list ruleset when you need the kernel's actual rule set.
