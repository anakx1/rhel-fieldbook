You will not fix everything alone. A good escalation gives the next person a running start and protects your credibility.

  • Impact — who or what is affected, and how severe is it?
  • Timeline — when it started, what changed, what you did, and the result. Use timestamps with time zones.
  • Evidence — exact commands and raw output, not a summary that removes the error.
  • Current state — is service restored? Partial? Degraded? What is the workaround?
  • Hypotheses — what you have ruled out, and what remains.
  • Next action — what you are about to try, and the rollback plan.

After the incident, write a short postmortem while it is fresh: trigger, detection gap, response, root cause, and one or two changes that would prevent recurrence. Blameless does not mean evidence-free; it means focusing on systems and decisions rather than individuals.

One change at a time

If you change three things and the problem disappears, you do not know what fixed it — and you may have introduced two new problems. Change one variable, observe, then proceed.
