Every process has a PID, a parent PID, an owner, a state, and a command line. PID 1 on RHEL is systemd, which adopts or reaps orphaned processes and manages services. A process lineage is called a tree for a reason.

terminal · process inventory
ps -ef
ps aux
pgrep -a sshd
ps -eo pid,ppid,user,stat,%cpu,%mem,cmd --sort=-%cpu | head

ps -ef uses BSD-style full format. ps aux is the common BSD syntax. Both are fine; pick one and learn to read it. pgrep -a finds processes by name and prints the full command line.

StateMeaning
RRunning or runnable.
SInterruptible sleep, waiting for an event.
DUninterruptible sleep, often I/O. Cannot be killed until it returns.
TStopped by a job-control signal.
ZZombie: exited, waiting for its parent to reap it.

A single D state process can indicate a storage problem. A pile of Z processes usually points to a broken parent, not a broken child. The state column is diagnostic, not decorative.
