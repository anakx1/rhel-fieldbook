A foreground process owns your terminal input. A background job runs without blocking the prompt but may still be tied to the terminal session.

terminal · job control
sleep 300 &
jobs
fg %1
bg %1
kill %1

The ampersand starts a job in the background. Ctrl+Z suspends the current foreground job — it does not terminate it. bg resumes it in the background; fg brings it back. The percent sign addresses a job, not a PID.

nohup makes a command ignore SIGHUP and detaches it from the terminal's hangup behavior. It is useful for quick lab work, but it is not a service manager. Production workloads belong in a systemd unit, where restarts, logging, dependencies, and resource limits are explicit.

Use tmux for long sessions

SSH sessions drop. A terminal multiplexer such as tmux keeps the shell alive on the remote host and lets you reconnect. It is more reliable than hoping a background job survives.
