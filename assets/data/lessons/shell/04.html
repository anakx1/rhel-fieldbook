These commands are the equivalent of checking your pockets. They answer: who am I, where am I, and what is this machine doing?

terminal · first contact
whoami
hostname
pwd
date
uptime
echo "hello, RHEL"

whoami prints the effective user. hostname prints the host's name; on modern RHEL, hostnamectl gives richer context. pwd prints the current working directory. uptime shows how long the system has been running, how many users are logged in, and load averages over 1, 5, and 15 minutes.

echo looks trivial, and then you use it to test quoting, variables, and redirection for the rest of your career. Try the difference:

terminal · quoting matters
echo $HOME
echo "$HOME"
echo '$HOME'

Double quotes expand variables; single quotes preserve the literal text. This distinction becomes critical in scripts, where a space or a dollar sign can change the meaning of a command.
