RHEL is deliberately conservative. Features arrive later than in Fedora, but they arrive with a lifecycle, a support statement, and a migration path. That trade is the entire point.

ConcernWhat RHEL optimizes for
StabilityStable major-version interfaces. Security fixes are backported instead of forcing a disruptive upgrade.
LifecycleA published support window measured in years, with defined maintenance and extended-life phases.
EcosystemCertified hardware, ISV applications, cloud images, and a partner catalog.
SecuritySELinux enforcing by default, signed packages, crypto policies, and a compliance story.
OperationsPredictable defaults, automation-friendly tooling, and enterprise support channels.

RHEL is not "the only Linux." Fedora is its fast-moving upstream community project. CentOS Stream is the rolling preview that sits directly upstream of the next RHEL minor release. Rebuilds such as Rocky Linux and AlmaLinux track RHEL source and are excellent for practice, but they are not identical products and do not include Red Hat support.

Read the release, not the meme

Commands differ subtly across versions. RHEL 9 and RHEL 10 both use systemd, SELinux, and the same administration model, but RHEL 10 moves to DNF5, newer container tooling, and a different default crypto posture. In this fieldbook, commands are written to work on both unless a version difference is called out.
