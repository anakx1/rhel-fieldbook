The journal is the first place to look when a unit fails. Filters make it readable: by unit, by boot, by time, by priority, and by output format.

terminal · journal filters
journalctl -u sshd -n 50 --no-pager
journalctl -u fieldbook-backup.service --since today --no-pager
journalctl -p warning --since "1 hour ago" --no-pager
journalctl -b -1 --no-pager
journalctl --disk-usage

-b selects a boot, -p filters by syslog priority, and --since bounds time. A quiet journal is not always good news: the unit may never have started. Check systemctl status and the timer's last trigger before concluding that the job succeeded.

Do not vacuum blindly

journalctl --vacuum-size discards evidence. Confirm retention requirements and investigate a filling filesystem before deleting history.

RHEL 9 vs RHEL 10

The timer and journal commands are compatible. Newer systemd releases may add calendar syntax and directives; validate a unit on the oldest release you support before rolling it out everywhere.
